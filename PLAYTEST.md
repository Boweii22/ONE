# Stranger playtest

The WhatsApp experiment was a promising friend-group signal, not proof of stranger retention or willingness to pay.

## Test format

Recruit 30 strangers into one scheduled 20-minute session. Give every account three free Revenge Tickets. Do not explain beyond the first-screen sentence: **This person owns ONE. Tap to steal it.** Do not host, referee, or prompt after the session begins.

Invite the same people back the following day with one dethroned notification.

## Record

- Players who attempt one steal.
- Players who steal at least twice.
- Pairwise rivalries with three or more exchanges.
- Echo usage.
- Reactions per reign.
- Free cooldown waits versus ticket skips.
- Organic shares and new spectators.
- Day-one returners.
- Reports and moderation latency.

## Continue thresholds

- At least 30% attempt a steal.
- At least 20% of activated players retake.
- At least three independent rivalries form.
- At least five players voluntarily spend a limited test ticket.
- At least 20% return the following day.
- The session continues for ten minutes without the creator prompting it.

Do not ask testers whether they *would* pay. Observe whether they spend scarce test tickets at the emotional moment, then test a real low-priced pack with a small consenting cohort.

When sharing the WhatsApp experiment publicly, use aggregate numbers and anonymized, consented excerpts. Do not publish participant names or the raw transcript.
